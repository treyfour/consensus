Consensus · design challenge

Consensus remembers papers. It doesn’t remember research.

Every screen is excellent at the moment you are on it. Nothing carries forward. As a thread grows, the papers that mattered get buried. Not because they are lost, but because the reason you kept them was never recorded anywhere.

“A place to make notes as I’m searching, tags with notes… an explanation why certain ‘most relevant’ papers are picked over others. Impossible to tell.” from the user feedback in the brief

The work today

Where Consensus sits in a graduate research process

Eight stages, from “I have a vague question” to “I defend this to my advisor”. Consensus is strongest at 1–3, finding and screening. It hands off at 4 and never sees the researcher again.

STAGES 1–3 · OWNED

Frame · Search · Screen. The funnel, per-query references, the compare matrix. All shipped, all good, and none of it is what I touched.

STAGES 4–5 · HANDED OFF

Read · Synthesise. Where the thinking actually happens, and where the researcher leaves for Zotero, Notion, or a document.

STAGES 6–7 · UNOWNED

Write · Defend. Nobody in this market owns the moment you have to justify a set of papers to someone else.

THE OBSERVATION THAT SET THE DIRECTION

The pain is not at stage 4 onward. It is that stages 1–3 produce nothing a researcher can carry into 4. Fixing the thread fixes the handoff as a side effect. Building a better library does not fix the thread.

THE OPEN SPACE

Nobody surfaces recurrence across independent searches as a relevance signal. A paper found by four unrelated queries is telling you something, and no product in this set shows it. And nobody connects the reason you kept a paper to the paper itself. That join is empty.

What I chose to focus on, and why

Organisation and control, not better retrieval

Pain 01, “why is this paper here”, is the loudest complaint in the feedback. I deliberately did not make it the centre of the work. Here is the reasoning, because it is the decision I most expect to be challenged on.

IMPROVING RELEVANCE

A retrieval and ranking problem. Better papers, better ordering, fewer near-duplicates. This is model and infrastructure work measured in quarters, and design cannot move it much. Betting the concept on it means the concept is only as good as a roadmap I do not control.

EXPLAINING RELEVANCE

Already computed, then thrown away. The agent trace holds three literal sub-queries with pool sizes, 31.7M · 656.6K · 442.8K, and the paper drawer holds 27 supporting quotes. All produced to write the answer, all discarded before they reach the paper. This costs a wire, not a model.

So: explain relevance, because it is nearly free. Do not try to fix it. Spend the design on the three pains where a researcher can act.

Organisation and control is where design actually moves the needle without a model change. It is also where the compounding is: a note is the only object that can carry a reason, and once reasons exist, everything else gets cheaper: asking a curated set, comparing two lines of enquiry, restoring what the Library drops.

THE ONE-LINE POSITION

My Library is what you kept. The note is why you kept it. Every rule in the concept follows from that sentence, and if a rule stops following from it, the rule is wrong.

Key concepts

One new object, and six rules it has to obey

The note is the only thing I added. Everything else is a relationship between things that already exist.

  • A note is thread-scoped by default. A note without a question is a Library entry, and a Library entry is the thing that already fails. No filing decision at capture time.
  • Nothing reaches My Library on its own. There is no single “Library” to save into. It is a tree of collections. So filing always names a destination, or nothing happens.
  • Ask is one verb. It does not navigate; it attaches what you have to the composer as scope, the way Papers · 20 attached already works.
  • Your own words are not part of the question unless you say so. Sources are published artefacts; your notes are you thinking out loud. Sources only by default, with one explicit opt-in.
  • Filing is a promotion, not a prerequisite. When a note outgrows one thread it becomes a collection: named, cross-thread, and arriving with its questions attached.
  • From a saved paper, you can see the notes and threads it came from. This is the return leg, and it is what makes the Library stop being a terminus.
THE CONSEQUENCE WORTH ARGUING ABOUT

A tag stops being only a way to find things in a note and becomes a way to ask part of one. Filter to ch.3 and the button reads Ask 4 of 9. The question is scoped to what you filtered.

Key flows

Five acts, one continuous loop

01AskThe funnel, unchanged, and expandable to the three sub-queries it already ran
02Why is this here?Hover any reference: the passage that matched, in the paper’s own words
03Keep what mattersDrag a set into the note. One note, nothing to file into
04Ask what you keptThe note attaches as scope, and the answer surfaces a paper you had not kept
05Come back, and acrossFile it, then ask two notes from two threads in one question
THE BEAT THAT PAYS

Act 4 is the one to watch. Everything before it is capture; everything after is filing. Act 4 is the only moment the note pays you back. The pile you built surfaces a paper you would otherwise have missed, which then joins the pile. If that lands, the rest follows. If it does not, the note is just a folder.

Tradeoffs, open questions, what I’d test next

What I traded away, and what I don’t know

TRADED AWAY

One note per thread, not many. No filing decision at capture, but organisation has to come from tags and filter rather than from structure.

Notes are one tab deep in My Library. The most personal content is not what you see on arrival. Softened by links into it from both other tabs.

Explaining relevance, not improving it. A researcher who wants better papers is not served by this work.

THINGS I BUILT AND REJECTED

A scope fence across the thread. Per-query References already answers “what am I searching against”.

Sections and sub-threads. The composer already shows the scope more plainly and holds it for longer.

Automatic seam detection. Measured: paper overlap scores 0.50–1.00 and the real subject change scores worse than a false one. It would confidently propose the wrong cut.

What I’d test next, in order

  • Does act 4 land? If the scoped answer surfacing a missed paper does not feel like a payoff, the whole concept is a folder with extra steps.
  • Is one note enough at forty papers? Tags plus filter is my bet; it is untested past a dozen cards.
  • Does “not what you needed?” get used, or does it feel like blame? It asks the goal rather than the fault specifically to avoid that, and that is a guess.
  • Is the private-by-default note the right call? The opposite is defensible, and would need saying out loud.
