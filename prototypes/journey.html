Problem framing · the journey, and the feedback

Consensus’s own account of the journey ends with the user leaving

They describe researchers coming here to explore, and then going elsewhere to save, organise and write. All seven things users asked to improve land at or before that handoff. Not one asks for a better reference manager. That is why this is a thread problem, not a library problem.

THE JOURNEY, IN CONSENSUS’S WORDS · THE SPINE OF THIS PAGE “Typically many researchers come to Consensus first in their process to get up to speed on a new topic, find the key papers, and in general explore the existing literature at the beginning of a new project. Once they have gone deep and found a core set of relevant papers, they may start to save and organize them in a reference manager and go on from there.”
THE FEEDBACK · SEVEN VERBATIM RESPONSES “What is the number one thing you would like to see improve about Consensus?”
Every quote on this page is one of the seven answers to that question, unedited. They are numbered ① – ⑦ and each is placed on the diagram at the point in the journey where it bites.

P1 · PROVENANCE

I can’t tell why this paper is here

Inside the sweep, returns when writing

4“Have an explanation why certain ‘most relevant’ papers are picked over others. Because now it’s impossible to tell.”Consensus user feedback
5“give me less but better pa[pers].”Consensus user feedback

Verified in product. The funnel is excellent, and aggregate: 32.8M → 100 → 20, with the three literal sub-queries and their pool sizes. It is attached to the answer, and to no individual paper.

P2 · ANNOTATION

I have nowhere to put a thought

The whole journey, and the only pain that crosses the handoff

1“Have a place to make notes as I’m searching, tags with notes so I can remember to check something later etc.”Consensus user feedback

Verified in product. No note, no tag, no highlight anywhere, thread or library. The paper drawer offers Overview, Snapshot, Attachment, Metadata, and no field that takes a sentence of yours.

P3 · CONTINUITY

Every question starts over

Across exploration, and every surface switch

6“try to use the same or similar papers in the follow up questions. It would be great to have flow in the references.”Consensus user feedback
7“It would be nice to have a way to combine queries and compare answers to have a more complex but easier-to-visualize information.”Consensus user feedback

Verified in product. Context flows forward and is never recorded: asking from the Citation Graph opens a top-level thread with no link back to the graph, the node, or the thread it came from.

P4 · THE HANDOFF

The paper survives, the question doesn’t

Exactly on the handoff, the crossing Consensus describes

3“What would help me most is saving time between finding papers and actually writing… so I can move faster from search to manuscript.”Consensus user feedback
2“…features that make the transition between searching and conceptualizing for a writing project easier through synthesizing the searches I have done on consensus.”Consensus user feedback

Verified in product. A saved paper keeps its authors, journal, SJR score, citation counts, DOI and full abstract. What it loses is the Evidence tab: the supporting quotes that existed only because you asked a question. Evidence is query-relative, and the Library has no query.

What this settles

Consensus describes the journey as explore here, then organise elsewhere. Read that way, the handoff is not a failure. It is the stated design. The feedback is what happens when users hit it: seven people asked for something to improve, and every one of them named work that happens at or before the crossing.

Three of the four pains sit inside exploration. The fourth is the crossing itself, and P2 is the only one that keeps hurting on the far side, because the thought a researcher had while reading is the one thing no reference manager can restore for them. None of the four needs a better retriever. They need the product to remember something it currently computes and throws away.

The papers make it across the handoff. The reasoning doesn’t. That is the whole problem, and it is a thread problem, which is why fixing the thread fixes the handoff as a side effect, and building a better library does not fix the thread.
