Where Consensus sits, and where the work breaks

Consensus is strong through the evidence sweep and stops at the seam. Three of the four pain points bite before the handoff, not after it. That is why this is a thread problem, not a library problem.

P1 · PROVENANCE

I can’t tell why this paper is here

Stage 3, returns at 7

“Have an explanation why certain ‘most relevant’ papers are picked over others. Because now it’s impossible to tell.”Consensus user feedback

P2 · ANNOTATION

I have nowhere to put a thought

Stages 1–5, worst at 4–5

“Have a place to make notes as I’m searching, tags with notes so I can remember to check something later.”Consensus user feedback

P3 · CONTINUITY

Every question starts over

Stages 1–3, and every surface switch

“Try to use the same or similar papers in the follow up questions. It would be great to have flow in the references.”Consensus user feedback

P4 · THE HANDOFF

The paper survives, the question doesn’t

Exactly at the seam, 3 → 4

“What would help me most is saving time between finding papers and actually writing… so I can move faster from search to manuscript.”Consensus user feedback

Verified in product. A saved paper keeps its authors, journal, SJR score, citation counts, DOI and full abstract. What it loses is the Evidence tab, the supporting quotes that existed only because you asked a question. Evidence is query-relative, and the Library has no query.

What this settles

Taking the brief at its word: as a thread grows, valuable papers get buried. Every pain below is a way that burial happens: a paper with no origin, no note, no memory between questions, and no trace of the question that surfaced it once it is saved.

The target is not stage 4 onward. Three of four pains sit at 1–3, inside the thread, and the fourth sits on the seam itself. Fixing the thread fixes the handoff as a side effect; building a better library does not fix the thread.
