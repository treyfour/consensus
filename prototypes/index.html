Wrong result → better search — four directions

Not “report a bad paper”. The click is a researcher saying this isn’t what I’m looking for, and the useful reply is a better search rather than a receipt. Four ways to catch that and turn it into progress, built on seven rules: ask the goal not the fault, never delete anything, make the click pay the user now, scale the response to the evidence, suggest but never fire, offer both scopes, and remember that a user who searches again and keeps nothing has already told you.

chosen: A · ask what they were after · the reason block is labelled WHY THIS PAPER in the accent, and the ask sits under it as a quiet not what you needed? · one accent per card, on the explanation rather than the exit · nothing strikes through, nothing is removed, and no suggested search fires on its own

Why is this source here? — five directions

One line, added to the source hover card that already exists. No new panel, no new surface, no new noun. The five differ in where that line comes from and what it costs to produce: assembled from what the pipeline already emitted, written with the answer in the same completion, written on hover and cached, the paper’s own matched passage, or no new sentence at all — hovering just highlights the answer sentence the paper holds up. Three of the five cost nothing extra to compute.

chosen: the paper’s own matched passage, plus a one-click ⚑ not relevant that records a specific signal before the user types anything · optional reasons after · undo at every step

AB · Why this one, and what if it’s wrong

Every reference explains itself in one line, and every reason in that explanation can be argued with. Press why ›, then ✕ not this on the part that is wrong. You never have to phrase the correction: the sub-query that found the paper is already a list of terms, so you point at delivery and the prototype shows what dropping it costs (which search gets rewritten, which references leave, which claim is withdrawn) before you commit. Two kinds of wrong are kept apart, because they teach different things: wrong paper reshapes the search, right paper, wrong claim leaves it alone. What Consensus records sits at the bottom of the pane, in the exact words it would be recorded in.

the positive signal is already free — keeping or citing a paper is a confirmation, so only the correction needs a control · free text is offered last, and admits that it changes nothing until a person reads it

Z · What isn’t there

References regrouped by the open question each paper answers instead of by rank, so a question being held up by one paper stops being invisible. Every group carries two numbers that are not the same number: how many papers the answer cited for it, and how many more are sitting nearby in your references, speaking to it, never cited. Press ↻ search this on a thin group and it runs a real query: if the group fills, you find out whether it filled from something new or from a paper you already had; and if it does not fill, it hardens from thin into gap. Absence stops being an observation and becomes a count of failed attempts.

the product already prints GAP — inside the answer, as a static figure that names no papers and dies with the message (screens/03) · this moves the same fact into References, keeps it live, and makes it falsifiable

Open →  ·  the reasoning: research/RELEVANCE.md TRACK A · ROUND 17

Y · What carries the answer

Rank tells you how well a paper matched the query. It does not tell you which three of twenty carry the answer. A claim here is one sentence plus the papers cited in it, and that is the whole computation: References splits into the papers that are the sole support of a claim and the papers that only corroborate. Click any reference to dim the answer to what it is holding, striking through the claims that are left with nothing. On the real ranks, the two top-ranked papers are the two the answer could lose; the four it cannot do without are #2, #4, #9 and #12.

the sort control (use · rank · recurrence) is the experiment, not the product: it exists to show where the orders disagree

Open →  ·  the reasoning: research/RELEVANCE.md ROUND 16 · THE WORKING PROTOTYPE

X · The working prototype

Not a walkthrough. Everything since round 11, assembled into something you can just use. It opens the way today's product looks, with Notes closed and a new badge on the button, so the feature has to earn its first click. Type a real query and get a real result set. Open and close either panel, and drop sources onto the Notes button while it is shut. Hover any citation to check the paper. Drag one source or a whole selection in. Write a note by typing at the top. Press Ask and the note attaches to the composer, answering in the same thread against your sources only. Tag as you go, then filter and ask just that subset. File it into a collection, and open it again from My Library.

recurrence is computed live: the dots count how many of your searches returned each paper · the union reports recurrence and no rank, because it genuinely has none · filtering by tag changes what Ask asks, so a tag is a way of asking part of a note

The other half of the loop, and the first prototype to leave the thread. A collection already holds Threads and Sources; Notes joins them as a peer rather than as a new surface with a new noun. Open →  ·  the reasoning: research/LOOP.md ROUND 13

U · Ask, and add to a collection

Ask is one verb at three sizes. It does not open anything; it attaches what you have to the composer as scope, the way Papers · N attached already works. That is what the note is for: you drag sources in over a session, then ask the whole set at once. Your written notes stay out of that scope unless you opt in, because some of them say things like “ask Sarah”. And nothing reaches My Library on its own any more — one uniform ＋ Add to collection button walks the real My Library › collection › sub-collection tree and refuses to accept anything at the root, so you always land somewhere you chose.

changed since round 12: “Ask this note” → “Ask”, attaching instead of spawning · the My Library and threads-asked counters are gone · the automatic Library link is reversed · one uniform Add to collection, with a breadcrumb drill-down · notes excluded from ask scope by default

 CONCEPT · COMPARING TWO LINES OF ENQUIRY

Compare two notes, in the Library we already built

Selecting two threads in My Library already works — the checkboxes and the 3 selected · ⋯ bar ship today (screens/20). What is missing is anything to do with the selection: you can chat against sources, but not against threads. A thread is 11 turns and 24 references; two of them will not fit in one question. A note is the reduction the researcher already performed — so notes are the unit that can be compared, and they carry the one thing sources alone cannot: the reasoning. The comparison names the paper both notes kept, quotes the researcher back to themselves, and says what neither note covers.

the disabled menu item is the argument: “chat against these threads · 19 turns · 41 references — too much to put in one question”, directly above “compare their notes · 7 cards · 5 sources”
