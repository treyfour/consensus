What I chose to work on, and why

Three of the four, and not the loudest one

All four pains are real. They do not cost the same to fix, and one of them is not a design problem at all. I picked the three where a researcher can act and where design moves the needle without a model change.

P1 · IMPROVING RELEVANCE

A retrieval problem, not a design one

Better papers, better ordering, fewer near-duplicates. This is model and infrastructure work measured in quarters, and design cannot move it much.

Betting the concept on it would make the concept only as good as a roadmap I do not control. So I left it alone.

P1 · EXPLAINING RELEVANCE

Already computed, then thrown away

The agent trace holds three literal sub-queries with their pool sizes, 31.7M · 656.6K · 442.8K, and the paper drawer holds 27 supporting quotes.

All of it is produced to write the answer and discarded before it reaches the paper. Surfacing it costs a wire, not a model. That part I did build.

Why this ordering

Three of the four pains sit inside the thread, and the fourth sits on the handoff between the thread and the reference manager. None of them need a better retriever. They need the product to remember something it currently throws away.

That is also where the compounding is. A note is the only object that can carry a reason, and once reasons exist the rest gets cheaper: asking a curated set, comparing two lines of enquiry, and restoring what the Library drops all become the same mechanism pointed in different directions.

My Library is what you kept. The note is why you kept it. Every rule in the concept follows from that sentence, and if a rule stops following from it, the rule is wrong.

Audited against the live product before designing: the retrieval funnel, per-query references, the Elicit-style compare matrix and source scoping all already ship and are good. Assumed rather than verified: the six finer journey stages, and that P1 returns at the writing desk. The two phases and the handoff are Consensus’s own words.
